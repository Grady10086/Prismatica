## Palette roles and materials

The new relationships are **stone and deep red**, **charcoal and warm ivory**,
**sand and restrained gold**, and **stone with violet and thin red accents**.
They complement the softer landscape directions in
[atmospheric composition](atmospheric-composition.md).

Use these as role descriptions, not sampled values or Pantone matches. Keep
the project's registered palette unless a change is requested; use paper-colors
to register any new theme. Red in these references is structural and decorative:
in a chart, red may already mean error, loss or a negative condition. Preserve
the scientific legend before borrowing the aesthetic.

Material contrast comes from rough versus smooth, opaque versus transparent,
and broad mass versus thin detail. In a website, express this with restrained
surface treatments, hairline edges and clear foreground/background separation.
Avoid texture behind small type and data marks. Metallic gradients are suitable
for a decorative form, not quantitative shading without a defined scale.

## Composition and typography

- A bright opening on one side creates depth and balances a dense foreground.
  Translate it into asymmetric panel allocation or a quiet margin with useful
  navigation; do not add empty manuscript panels to mimic architecture.
- Repeated blossoms connect foreground, middle distance and background through
  a shared accent. In a page, a repeated color or small motif can connect section
  markers, selected controls and call-to-action details without new imagery.
- The posters pair a large, light serif wordmark with small widely spaced
  metadata and narrow text columns. Borrow the scale contrast for editorial
  headings; retain legible horizontal labels and normal spacing for scientific
  annotations. Preserve Prismatica's logo and approved copy.
- Use a fine line to give a transparent surface a boundary. Check that the line
  remains visible at the actual display size and in static exports.

## Scientific and interactive applications

**Method overview:** an open geometric frame around one actual mechanism,
with a few well-spaced supporting modules. Connections represent documented
relationships. Floating fragments may inspire module placement, but node size,
edge width and position need explicit meaning when encoding measurements.

**Image-led teaser:** combine a dominant real result, a restrained accent area
and smaller evidence crops. Keep result imagery faithful; decorative side light,
reflections and material effects belong outside the experimental image layer.

**Website:** retain the current cool atmospheric base. Apply one compatible
architectural motif at a time: a fine open frame, an edge highlight, or a clearly
bounded translucent surface. Avoid combining red stone, gold metal, glass,
particles and violet accents into every section. Motion can use subtle highlight
or frame changes on hover, with no persistent particle field obscuring controls.
Honor the existing pause control and reduced-motion preference for any new
animated decoration; keyboard focus must have an equally clear static state.

**Data experiments:** borrow palette restraint and hierarchy, keeping a flat,
readable chart plane. Do not add dramatic lighting to heatmaps, make measured
connections sag like threads, mirror results as additional evidence, or render
uncertainty as falling particles. A decorative ripple is not a causal claim.

## Final-size checks

Confirm a single focal region, readable small labels, semantic colors consistent
across views, no occluded connections, and a clear distinction between evidence
and decorative material. A still screenshot and vector export should preserve
the same scientific interpretation as the interactive version.
