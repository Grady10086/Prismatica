### Three directions to choose from

**Mist and ink:** subdued blue-grey, blush and ivory with sparse dark contours.
Use when the main challenge is hierarchy in a dense method or experimental
composition. Keep the data layer precise even if the surrounding design is soft.

**Blue, ochre and ivory:** a clearer, brighter relationship from image 9, with
geometric and organic forms balancing one another. Useful for architecture
plus real-result layouts. Reuse a compatible registered palette, or register
an explicitly requested new palette through paper-colors; Iris does not itself
provide a literal golden-ochre match.

**Teal and coral:** a broad cool field balanced by a warm mass, with a small
dark anchor, as in image 10. Useful for image-led covers and teasers. For a
multi-series experiment, translate this into two semantic color families and
neutral context rather than tinting every input image or series.

These are role-based directions, not sampled palettes. Match saturation and
lightness as well as hue: blue and orange alone do not reproduce image 9's
balance, and teal and coral need enough neutral separation to remain legible.

### Composition choices without a fixed template

- A horizontal spine with aligned evidence below can borrow the ledge's
  structural clarity. Keep panel borders light so the alignment does the work.
- A larger geometric overview beside a detailed result mosaic can borrow the
  house/tree balance. Match crop scales wherever the scientific comparison
  depends on object size.
- A primary image with a narrow row of measured evidence can borrow the
  cloud/horizon balance. Allocate the sky-like quiet field only when it serves
  a title or real visual content; do not enlarge manuscript whitespace to
  imitate the reference's proportions.
- A conceptual branching overview can taper from coarse to fine, but diagram
  stroke weight, node size and flow width need a declared meaning if they
  encode quantities. Decorative repeated marks must not look like extra data.

Do not use the artistic reflections as permission to mirror experimental
images, duplicate observations or imply an additional measured condition.

### Color and density options

The coastal images offer **cyan, sand and a compact yellow accent**; the cactus
offers **slate, beige and charcoal**; the canopy images offer **ochre or moss,
dark bark and blue gaps**. Treat these as optional role relationships. Retain
the current registered theme unless a palette change is requested. Any new
palette needs explicit color values and contrast checks through paper-colors;
these descriptions are not sampled colors or Pantone identifications.

The forest views show that a strong composition can be dense. Distinguish a
large primary structure from repeated secondary ones through scale, spacing
and clear gaps. For a dense experiment plate, use a prominent main comparison
and closely aligned supporting panels rather than extra decorative branches.
Avoid forced perspective in quantitative plots: apparent size must not alter
how values or comparable image content are judged.

### Layering, arcs and visual correspondence

- Use translucent shapes for conceptual surfaces or separable decorative
  layers. If overlap represents set membership, probability or uncertainty,
  use the actual data and an explicit legend. Alpha blending creates extra
  perceived colors and is unsuitable as an unexplained category encoding.
- Use open arcs to frame content in a cover, teaser or conceptual overview.
  A frame's curvature does not imply cyclic data or a measured connection.
  Do not bend an ordinary quantitative axis to fit the crescent motif.
- Carry semantic colors and aligned anchors between overview and evidence.
  Artistic reflection is a composition reference; experimental images retain
  their true orientation, and a repeated view is identified as the same view.
- Keep soft edges in optional backgrounds or conceptual materials. Data
  boundaries, uncertainty limits, axes and result crops remain precise.

## Color roles

- Use a neutral paper field, a subdued cool family (blue-grey or grey-green),
  and a warm family (blush, peach or muted coral). Give each a distinct role.
- Reserve charcoal and the strongest warm accent for the focal evidence or
  annotation. A mostly quiet palette makes a small accent effective.
- Preserve the registered project palette. With Iris, use `#758E8B` and
  `#55609B` for cool roles, `#E6A385` for warmth and `#AB6690` sparingly.
  These are existing project colors, not colors identified from the artwork.
- Light tints can organize a panel background; plotted marks and text still
  need sufficient contrast. Check tints against the actual export background.
- For quantitative colormaps, select a perceptually ordered ramp with the
  proper sequential/diverging meaning. Do not use a sunset gradient just
  because its endpoints are attractive. Keep categorical identities stable.

Use the sibling paper-colors workflow for palette registration and exports.
Do not assign Pantone codes to screenshot colors by visual approximation.

## Translate the composition by output

### Experimental figure

Choose a primary comparison and give it the largest useful panel. Place two
or three supporting views along a clear reading path: overall behavior,
condition-specific evidence, then the cases that explain the difference.
Allocate space to labels and evidence density, not decorative color blocks.

Keep axes crisp, error intervals defined and all comparison series visible.
Express hierarchy through panel size, annotation and line weight. Do not fade
a baseline until its trend or uncertainty becomes difficult to read. Share
scales where comparison requires them; never move measured points to form a
more attractive curve.

### Trees, trajectories and spatial biology

The receding trees suggest how line hierarchy can clarify dense structure.
Use actual branch structure or trajectory order; encode branch weight only
when its meaning is specified. Keep spatial coordinates, topology and sample
identities intact. A pale contextual layer can support crisp selected marks,
with selection linked to the same items in the companion panel.

Do not turn atmospheric softness into blurred spatial evidence, remove minor
branches for decoration or imply unmeasured uncertainty through a mist effect.

### Teaser, method overview or project page

Use an original, restrained cool–warm field behind crisp real-result crops.
An asymmetric main result with smaller comparison insets can replace uniform
cards. A route through panels may echo a shoreline, provided arrows represent
actual relationships. Localize atmospheric texture away from labels and data.
Keep decorative backgrounds separable from the editable scientific layers.

## Review at final size

- Is the main finding the strongest focal point, rather than a decorative shape?
- Does each quiet region separate groups, support labels or guide reading?
  The references use expansive atmosphere; manuscript panels need less space.
- Are cool/warm colors consistent with the legend, including negative results?
- Are fine contours and small annotations readable in the final PDF and PNG?
- Does the figure preserve all values, uncertainty, relationships and scales?

The aim is deliberate contrast between calm fields and precise evidence.
Do not apply painterly texture to every chart or make every layout sparse.
